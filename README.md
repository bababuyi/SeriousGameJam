# Two Weeks Notice (Chair Spin) — Office Chair Combat Game

An Unreal Engine 5 Blueprint-only game built for the **Serious Game Jam**. You play an office worker strapped into a wheeled chair, building rotational momentum to survive a hostile office. Spin fast enough and you are a weapon. Spin too much and the whole building notices.

---

## Overview

**Two Weeks Notice** is a chaos-physics arena game about momentum management. The chair does not have a normal movement controller: it has a velocity vector and a spin value that you push, drag, and ricochet around an office floor. Your spin speed is simultaneously your damage output, your defence, and the thing that makes the office want you dead.

Every action you take generates **Heat**. Heat is what wakes up coworkers, brings the Manager down on you, and turns the neutral Janitor into a hazard. The result is a loop where going fast is always tempting and always expensive.

---

## Core Loop

```
Spin up → become lethal → generate Heat → the office aggros
  → dodge, kick, use pickups → let Heat decay → repeat
```

- **Maintain spin or be vulnerable.** Above the spin threshold you damage enemies on contact. Below it, they damage you.
- **Chaos has a cost.** Heat is generated by speed and by bumping into things, and it is the aggro gate for every enemy in the game.
- **The office is reactive, not aggressive.** Enemies wander until you provoke them. The Janitor never chases you at all unless you have made a mess.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| **WASD** | Push the chair (impulse, not direct movement) |
| **Q / E** | Spin left / right |

## Key Systems

### Custom Chair Movement (`PC`)
The CharacterMovementComponent is disabled at BeginPlay (`SetMovementMode(MOVE_None)`). All motion is integrated manually each tick from two variables:

- `Velocity` (vector) with per-tick `DirectionalDrag`
- `RotationalVelocity` (float) with `RotationalDrag`, clamped by `MaxRotationalSpeed`

Movement is applied with a swept `SetActorLocation` so the capsule still blocks against geometry, while the visual chair mesh spins independently via `AddLocalRotation`. A dedicated "Directional Fix" arrow defines forward, because the actor root deliberately never rotates.

Tick is split into named helper functions rather than one graph: `Movement`, `CameraMovement`, `HeatGeneration`, `BladeUpdate`, `MopUpdate`.

**Surface interactions:**
- **Wall boost** — kicking into a wall reflects your velocity (`GetReflectionVector`) and scales it by `WallBoostStength`
- **Floor boost** — `FloorBoostStrength` applied off ground contact
- **Wet floor** — overlapping `BP_WetFloor` multiplies velocity down by `SlowFactor` each tick
- **Ice / Bouncy power-ups** — temporary friction and restitution modifiers, cleared by timer (`Clear Ice`, `ClearBounce`)

### Kick System
A charged sphere trace from the chair's forward arrow. Holding builds the kick (`isChargingKick`) with a global time dilation slow-mo on release; hitting a wall converts into a boost, hitting an enemy applies knockback and burst damage. Gated by `CanKick` / `KickCooldown`.

### Heat & Aggro (`GS_Serious`)
A custom `GameStateBase` holds all global state:

| Variable | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `Heat`, `MaxHeat`, `HeatDecayRate` | Global aggro pressure, clamped and decayed on tick |
| `ShiftLength`, `ShiftTimeRemaining`, `ShiftEnded` | Shift clock |
| `Total`, `HighScore`, `SlotName` | Score and persistent high score |
| `All checkpoints`, `Current CheckPoint Index`, `AmountOfCheckPoints` | Objective routing |

Heat is added by `AddHeat` from two sources: sustained spin/velocity (`VelocityHeatRate`) and collisions (`BumpHeat`). Every enemy reads `GS_Ref.Heat` against its own `AggroHeatThreshHold`, so one shared value drives the entire office's behaviour.

### Enemy State Machine (`BP_EnemyBase`)
All enemies are `Character` + `AIController` pawns navigating a NavMesh with `AI MoveTo`. One base class drives every enemy type through **data variance on Class Defaults**, not separate logic:

- **Timer-driven, not tick-driven.** `UpdateChase` runs on a looping 0.25s timer. Calling `AI MoveTo` every frame restarts the path and stutters.
- **`UpdatePerception`** flips state to Chase on either a vision-cone dot product test within `SightRange`, or on `Heat >= AggroHeatThreshHold`. It drops back to Idle past `LoseSightRange`.
- **Idle wander** picks `GetRandomReachablePointInRadius` targets inside `WanderRadius`.
- **Kiting** (`Ranged = true`): advances to `PreferredRange` and holds, retreats by `BackupDistance` if the player closes inside `FleeRange`.
- **Grab** (`Grab = true`): damps player `Velocity` and `RotationalVelocity` toward zero with `VInterpTo` / `FInterpTo` at `GrabStrength`.
- **Reinforcements**: spawns `ReinforcementClass` on `ReinforcementCooldown` up to `MaxReinforcements`, with a lifetime `SpawnedCount` cap so it cannot flood.
- **Death**: reports `ScoreValue` to `AddKill`, then ragdolls (`SetSimulatePhysics` + `HideBoneByName`) rather than simply despawning.

### Enemy Roster

| Enemy | Identity | Key settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Coworker** | Wandering chaser that grabs and slows you | `canWander`, `Grab`, Health 30, Speed 300, ContactDPS 50, AggroHeat 60 |
| **Intern** | Ranged kiter firing paper planes, backs off if you charge | `Ranged`, ShootRange 1000, Cooldown 1.5, PreferredRange 700, FleeRange 400 |
| **Manager** | Slow, low Heat threshold, calls in reinforcements | Speed 200, Health 120, AggroHeat 30, Reinforcements `BP_Coworker` x4 |
| **Janitor** | Neutral hazard, see below | Perception disabled by data |

### The Janitor (`BP_Janitor`)
An unkillable neutral NPC and the game's built-in cost for running hot. He is made neutral **through data alone** (`SightRange = 0`, `VisionConeRange = 0`, `AggroHeatThreshHold = 999999`) so the base perception can never flip him hostile, and his own patrol logic drives him instead.

- **Patrol** loops a `PatrolPoints` array via delegate-driven `AI MoveTo`, advancing `(PatrolIndex + 1) % Length` on success.
- **Cleaning** takes priority: he searches for `BP_WetFloor` actors, moves to the nearest, destroys it, then resumes his route.
- **Provoked attacks** trigger only at `Heat >= MenaceHeat` while you are in range. All three are telegraphed with a `WindupTime` dodge window followed by a `RecoverTime` punish window:

| Range | Attack | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `PourRange` (~250) | **Pour Water** | Spawns a `BP_WetFloor` slip hazard at his feet |
| `MopRange` (~450) | **Mop Swing** | Re-checks range after windup, cuts velocity and spin by `MopSlowFactor`, shoves and damages |
| `TrashRange` (~1200) | **Trash Bag** | Lobs a slow arcing projectile at your post-windup position |

### Weapons & Pickups
`OwnedWeapons` is a **Set** of `Enum_Weapons`, so multiple weapons can be held and fired at once. `BP_WeaponPickup` adds a weapon on overlap and starts a timer that removes it again after `WeaponDuration`, making every weapon a temporary state rather than an inventory slot.

| Weapon | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Paper Cutter** | Straight-line thrown projectile |
| **Paper Plane** | Thrown projectile, fires alongside the Cutter |
| **Blades** | Disc under the chair that spins independently; both damage and spin rate scale off the player's own `RotationalVelocity` (`BladeDamageScale`, `BladeSpinScale`) |
| **Mop** | Wide push aura that launches overlapping enemies away |

### Spawn Manager (`BP_SpawnManager`)
Timer-driven spawner for both enemies and pickups. Maintains live counts against `MaxEnemy` / `MaxPickup`, picks positions with `GetRandomLocationInNavigableRadius` inside `SpawnRadius`, and respawns pickups on `PickupRespawnDelay`.

### Checkpoints (`BP_CheckPoint`)
Objective markers with Niagara visuals. `GS_Serious` collects all checkpoints on BeginPlay, activates one at a time, and rerolls the next index on completion (`Reroll`, `Set Next CheckPoint`), with an `isProcessingCheckPoint` guard against double-fires.

### Editor Tooling (`MovableToStaticMesh`)
An **Editor Utility Widget** built to speed up level dressing. It reads the current level selection and converts actors in both directions:

- Static Mesh Actor → `BP_Movable` physics prop, carrying the mesh and transform across and applying a mass override
- `BP_Movable` → plain Static Mesh Actor

This turned "make this prop kickable" from a manual per-actor job into a two-click operation for the level artists.

## Development Notes

A short record of the non-obvious problems this build ran into, kept because most of them cost real jam hours.

### The actor root never rotates
`AddLocalRotation` targets the chair's visual mesh only, so `GetActorForwardVector` always points in the spawn direction. Every piece of directional logic (kick trace, weapon spawn rotation, push direction) has to read from the Directional Fix arrow instead. Thrown weapons fired backwards until that vector stopped being negated.

### Set Timer with Time <= 0 clears the timer
It does not fire immediately, it silently cancels. Any cooldown driven off a variable needs a non-zero default. Weapon pickups expired the instant they were collected because `WeaponDuration` was not wired into the timer.

### Hidden in Game overrides SetVisibility
A component with `Hidden in Game = true` stays invisible regardless of `SetVisibility` calls. This presented as "the Mop won't spawn" and cost far longer to find than it should have.

### AI MoveTo fails silently
An empty `Pawn` pin does nothing at all, with no warning. So does a destination that lands off the NavMesh or behind geometry, which is why kiting retreat targets use modest `BackupDistance` offsets rather than large ones.

### Launch Character does nothing to the player
Because the chair's CMC is disabled, the only way to shove the player is to write directly to its `Velocity` variable. The Mop's push aura calls `Launch Character` on the **enemy**, and initially launched the player instead because the target pin was left on Self.

### Enemies were reparented from Actor to Character
NavMesh pathfinding requires a `Character` with an `AIController` and `Auto Possess AI` set. The original Actor-based enemies moved by `SetActorLocation` and walked straight through walls.

### Damage ordering
`TakeDamage` sets Health first, then tests the **Set node's output pin** against zero. Feeding the subtract node into the comparison instead causes the pure node to re-evaluate and produce inconsistent death checks.
